# Beyond Ukraine: how the drone war spread, 2025–26

Research note for KILL ZONE. Compiled 14 September 2026.

**What this is for.** The game's five-channel detection model was built around a
Ukraine-style front: two armies pressed against each other along a narrow
contact line, saturated with cheap sensors and cheap munitions. This document
asks what changes if an alternative campaign is set somewhere else — NATO's
eastern flank, the Gulf, the first island chain. The short answer is that the
*sensor physics* travel unchanged but almost everything around them — density,
tempo, who owns the sky, what a "turn" costs — does not.

**How to read the confidence markers.** Every substantive claim is tagged:

- **[Reported]** — a specific event or figure carried by named reporting.
- **[Announced]** — a government or alliance said it will do something. Not the
  same as it existing. This distinction is doing a lot of work in this document.
- **[Inferred]** — my reasoning from the above. Not sourced.

Confidence is **[High] / [Medium] / [Low]**.

**Research limits, stated up front.** Direct page fetches are blocked by network
policy, so **everything here is as summarised in search-result snippets rather
than read in full.** No citations have been invented to fill gaps, and where a
search returned nothing the document says so.

**Second research pass, 14 September 2026.** The first version of this document
was written with the session's search budget nearly exhausted. A second pass
added: the **2026 Iran war** (section 5), which the first version did not cover
at all and which is the largest omission it had; a rewritten **section 7** on
Taiwan and the Pacific, now Medium rather than Low confidence; a new **section
12** setting out which practices transferred and which did not; and a new
**section 13** stating explicitly where KILL ZONE is at risk of being overfitted
to one conflict. Sections 3, 4, 6, 9, 10 and 11 gained material. **Where the

## 1. Summary

**The drone war spread as two different things, not one.** [High] What reached
NATO's eastern flank is the *long-range one-way attack drone* and the political
problem of what to do about it. What reached the Middle East, Africa and Latin
America is that plus the *cheap tactical quadcopter*. The trench-line FPV duel
that defines the Ukraine front has not been reproduced anywhere at comparable
density, because nowhere else has two industrialised armies dug in fifteen
metres apart.

**NATO's eastern flank is being probed, not attacked.** [Reported, High]
Romania recorded 33 airspace incursions since 2022, 18 of them in 2026 alone as
of August. One analysis tracked 30 suspected or confirmed violations between
September 2025 and January 2026, against 23 in the whole preceding period from
March 2022 to August 2025. The rate has stepped up by roughly an order of
magnitude. Nobody has been killed by one on NATO soil in a way that forced
Article 5.

**The alliance's real answer so far is a truck with small interceptor drones on
it, not a wall.** [Reported, High] Merops — built by the US firm Perennial
Autonomy, formerly Project Eagle — went to Poland and Romania from November
2025, at roughly $14,500 per interceptor, against Sidewinders at over €400,000
a shot. That is the single most consequential fact in this document, and the
game should treat it as a unit type, not a detail.

**The "drone wall" is mostly an announcement.** [Announced / High confidence
that it is an announcement] The EU's European Drone Defence Initiative and the
Finland/Poland-led Eastern Flank Watch have timelines (initial capacity end
2026, completion end 2027 per the EU defence commissioner; other framings say
full function end 2028) and funding instruments (SAFE loans), and as of July
2026 critics were publicly noting that ten months after it was promised it did
not exist. A Baltic consortium's funding request was rejected in March 2026
without published reason.

**Ukraine is now an exporter of doctrine and IP, on deliberately asymmetric
terms.** [Reported, High] Under the "Drone Deal" framework Ukraine supplies the
intellectual property and combat experience; partners supply capital and
factory floor. Agreements exist with the Netherlands, Lithuania, Latvia,
Denmark, Estonia and Norway. In July 2026 Ukraine cut its own transfer approval
process to 30 days, and signed a first statement of intent with the United
States.

**The cost-exchange problem is the organising fact of the whole thing.** [High]
Roughly $1bn of US naval interceptors were burned in the Red Sea at about
$4.5m per missile average against targets worth thousands. Every procurement
decision since is downstream of that number.

**Proliferation is effectively unstoppable at the low end.** [High] The RSF is
flying long-range one-way attack drones with something in the 2,000 km class
across Sudan. Cartels, Sahelian insurgents and Myanmar resistance factions all
field FPV. The skill, not the hardware, is the constraint — and the skill is
being taught.

**Is Western procurement fast enough? No, and not close.** [Inferred, Medium]
What arrived quickly arrived because single states used unusual acquisition
authorities. Everything routed through multinational instruments is measured in
years against a threat that iterates in weeks.

### Added on the second pass

**There was a second real drone war in 2026, and this document missed it.**
[Reported, High] The **2026 Iran war** ran from roughly late February 2026 and
was still going in September. Iran struck US bases and Gulf states with one-way
attack drones and ballistic missiles; Kuwait reports **97 ballistic missiles and
283 drones shot down**, Bahrain **174 missiles and 391 drones intercepted** since
the war began. A Shahed hit a US tactical operations centre at **Port Shuaiba,
Kuwait on 1 March 2026**, killing six US Army Reserve soldiers - air defence
failed to intercept it. **This, not the NATO flank, is the closest thing to a
peer air-defence-versus-drone campaign outside Ukraine**, and the game should be
built against it as much as against Ukraine.

**The cost-exchange problem stopped being theoretical and ate the magazine.**
[Reported, High] The Iran war consumed an estimated **65-80% of the US Patriot
interceptor stockpile**, from a CSIS-estimated pre-war inventory of roughly
**2,200 modern Patriots and 452 THAAD**. Restocking is described as a
multi-year problem. A Shahed-136 costs around **$50,000**; a Patriot interceptor
around **$4 million**. Gulf states publicly worried that the shortage would
degrade their own defence. **Magazine depth is now the single most important
resource in any drone-defence scenario, and it is a campaign-length resource,
not a mission-length one.**

**Counter-drone money went vertical.** [Reported, High] Publicly announced
government spending on counter-UAS reached **~$29bn in the first three months of
2026** and **over $53bn in the first eight months**. The US Army's **$20bn
ten-year enterprise contract with Anduril (March 2026)** is described as the
largest published C-UAS contract in the world. NATO allies announced **$40bn+
over five years**; Poland's SAN CUAS programme is **$4.2bn**; Australia up to
**A$7bn**. Compare the total EU "drone wall" framing of €1.26bn across 57
projects. **The wall is not where the money is. Point defence is.**

**Acoustic detection is the Ukrainian technology that actually transferred, and
this document previously said the opposite.** [Reported, Medium-High -
**correction to the first version**, which recommended weakening acoustic on the
grounds that "the dense Ukrainian acoustic net has no flank equivalent yet."]
Ukraine's **Sky Fortress** acoustic network is described as running **10,000+
sensors** in 2026, with **NATO funding a further 15,000 third-generation
sensors**. **Lithuania will deploy Sky Fortress in 2026** - reported as the first
direct transfer of a combat-proven Ukrainian system to a NATO member - and the US
military deployed the platform at **Prince Sultan Air Base, Saudi Arabia** in
April 2026 against Shaheds. Acoustic is cheap, passive and networkable, which is
exactly why it travels.

**Fibre-optic control travelled to a non-state actor inside about a year, and it
is no longer immune to everything.** [Reported, Medium-High] Since **March 2026**
fibre-optic FPVs have been Hezbollah's prominent weapon against the IDF in
southern Lebanon: **80+ launched, ~15 hits, 4 soldiers and a civilian killed** in
one recent period - a **~19% hit rate**, which is a far more useful number than
any vendor claim. Meanwhile **Epirus demonstrated a high-power microwave kill on
a fibre-optic drone in January 2026**. A fibre link defeats jamming; it does not
defeat an HPM that cooks the airframe's electronics regardless of how it is
controlled.

**The production asymmetry is the fact that should worry a designer most.**
[Reported, Medium-High] Ukraine plans **7 million drones in 2026** (up to
~200,000/month reported), Russia has fielded **1.5 million+ small drones**, and
the **US Army's target is 10,000/month by late 2026** with a million a year by
2028. Ukraine's planned output is roughly **seventy times** US annual combat
drone output. **Nobody outside this war fights at Ukrainian drone density, and
nobody is close to being able to.**

## 3. NATO's eastern flank

**The incursions.** [Reported, High] Poland and Romania take the bulk. The
pattern is spillover from strikes on Ukraine rather than deliberate attack —
navigation drift, deliberate routing through NATO airspace to complicate
intercepts, and probing. Romania's 33-since-2022 / 18-in-2026 split is the
clearest published measure of the acceleration. NATO's own language has shifted
from "violations" to Russia demonstrating "increasing tolerance for risk," which
is diplomatic phrasing for *deliberate*.

**What was deployed.** [Reported, High] Eastern Sentry is modest in hardware —
a few squadrons' worth of fighters, a frigate, helicopters, rotated by
contributors. Its significance is procedural: it made eastern-flank air defence
a collective NATO conduct rather than a national one.

**What was bought, and by whom.** [Reported, High]

- **Merops** (US, Perennial Autonomy). Truck-mounted, launches small interceptor
  drones. Reported performance: roughly 5–20 km range, up to 280 km/h, 2 kg
  fragmentation warhead, terminal homing on RF emissions, radar return **or**
  thermal signature. **~$14,500 per interceptor.** Poland and Romania from
  November 2025; subsequently integrated with various radars; subsequently
  deployed to the Middle East against Shahed-pattern drones.
- **Fighters with air-to-air missiles.** Still the default for anything that has
  to be killed *now*, at €400k+ a shot.
- **National walls.** Poland's border/air-defence programme and Romania's
  buildup draw on EU SAFE long-maturity loans.

**What exists versus what was announced.** [High] This is the load-bearing
distinction for a game designer.

*Exists:* Eastern Sentry's rotational air presence; dozens of Merops systems in
two countries; a trained cadre; an experimentation exercise (Eastern Phoenix 26)
that put ~215 systems in one place to see what works; radar integration work.

*Announced:* the European Drone Defence Initiative / "drone wall"; Eastern Flank
Watch as an integrated air-defence, EW, surveillance and maritime picture along
the whole border; €1.26bn across 57 defence projects; initial capacity "end
2026," completion "end 2027" or "end 2028" depending on the statement. As of
July 2026 the public assessment was that the wall did not exist.

*Inference [Medium]:* what the flank actually has in 2026 is **point defence at
a handful of sites plus alert fighters**, not area coverage. A drone that does
not fly over a defended point is not engaged. That is a very different tactical
picture from Ukraine, where sensor coverage along the contact line is close to
continuous.

### Added on the second pass

**EDDI did launch, and it is not a wall.** [Announced/Reported, Medium] The
European Drone Defence Initiative was launched in **Q1 2026**. Its published
description is **interoperable networks of sensors, electronic warfare tools and
interceptor systems** connected between member states and aligned with NATO
command structures — explicitly *not* a physical barrier. Initial capacity end
2026, full function end 2027; **EDIP allocates €1.5bn across 2026-27**. As of
September 2026 it is described as "approaching" initial operational capacity. The
Commission also published an **Action Plan on Drone and Counter-Drone Security**
in February 2026. **Correction to the first version's framing:** the wall is not
vapour, it is a different thing from what the name suggested — a network
programme whose output is sensor and C2 integration, not coverage.

**National spending dwarfs the collective instrument.** [Reported, High]
**Poland's SAN CUAS programme is ~$4.2bn** on its own. NATO allies collectively
announced **$40bn+ over five years** for counter-drone capabilities. Against
that, €1.26bn across 57 EU projects is a rounding error. **The money is
national, and it is going into point defence.**

**The civil airspace problem is now a front of its own.** [Reported, High] From
September 2025 a wave of unattributed drone sightings closed civil airports:
Copenhagen (~4 hours, 77 cancellations, 217 delays), Munich on consecutive days,
Brussels twice in one night on 4 November, Berlin Brandenburg for two hours.
Denmark logged **107 illegal drone flights in 2025**. Belgium reported ten
sightings in one week including **drones of different sizes flying in
formation**, and committed ~€50m plus a National Airspace Security Centre
from 1 January 2026. Denmark deployed a XENTA C-UAS radar to Copenhagen airport.

*Inference [Medium-High]:* **the eastern-flank scenario is not a military map, it
is a civil one.** The defended points are airports, ports, power stations and
bases inside a population, and the constraint on shooting is legal and political
before it is technical. That is a genuinely different game from a contact line.

**Theatre-scale GNSS denial is already a lived condition on NATO territory.**
[Reported, Medium-High] Russian jamming from Kaliningrad and around St Petersburg
is assessed to have affected **60,000+ commercial flights**. On **25 August 2026**
reporting described "phones, drones and ships running blind as GPS malfunctions
across Poland." Russian EW in Kaliningrad is separately assessed as **redirecting
Ukrainian drones toward Finland and the Baltic states** — which is how a drone
launched by one belligerent becomes a NATO airspace violation attributed to
another. For comparison, Ukraine-front jamming is described as creating
**persistent GPS degradation zones 50-200 km deep from the line** [single
snippet, Low — the source could not be assessed]. **The difference is not
intensity; it is that on the flank the denial is permanent, ambient and
unattributable rather than tactical.**

## 4. Ukrainian-origin systems adopted elsewhere, and the terms

[Reported, High] The **Drone Deal** framework is explicit about the trade:
Ukraine contributes IP and combat-proven designs; the partner contributes
industrial capacity and capital; production is localised in the partner country
or jointly in Ukraine. Signed or in implementation with the **Netherlands,
Lithuania, Latvia, Denmark, Estonia and Norway**. Denmark's version builds on
the earlier "Danish model" of directly financing Ukrainian manufacturers. Norway
planned a pilot production line for 2026 with scaling thereafter.

[Reported, High] In July 2026 Ukraine cut its transfer approval procedure to
**30 days** — a deliberate move to make itself a fast supplier rather than a
bureaucratic one.

[Reported, High] The **US** relationship is newer and narrower: a statement of
intent, not a production contract. Six firms permitted to export roughly 100
drones each for test and evaluation; one firm reported shipping 2,000 units
under a first export permit. This feeds the Pentagon's "drone dominance"
push — reported as a $1bn programme for 200,000 drones by 2027.

[Inferred, Medium] The interesting term is the *direction of the IP*. Ukraine is
not selling finished units at scale; it is selling the right to build, plus the
tacit knowledge of what fails. That is a different commercial shape from arms
export and it is why the agreements multiplied so fast — they cost the partner
capital rather than needing Ukrainian factory output that Ukraine cannot spare.

### The system that actually transferred: Sky Fortress [Reported, Medium-High — added on the second pass]

Of everything Ukraine has built, the item with the clearest documented transfer
to other theatres is not a drone. It is an **acoustic sensor network**.

- **Sky Fortress** has been deployed since 2022 and is described as running
  **more than 10,000 acoustic sensors** across Ukraine as of 2026. The sensors
  are microphones and smartphones on poles, with machine learning picking the
  characteristic sound of a low-flying drone out of the background. It was built
  first against Shahed-136s.
- **NATO has committed funding for 15,000 third-generation sensors** across
  Ukraine.
- **Lithuania will deploy Sky Fortress in 2026** — reported as the **first
  direct transfer of a combat-proven Ukrainian system to a NATO member**.
- As of **April 2026** the platform was deployed by the US military at **Prince
  Sultan Air Base, Saudi Arabia**, as part of Shahed defence during the Iran war.
- There is published advocacy for adopting it along NATO's **Eastern Flank
  Deterrence Line** and in **INDOPACOM**.

**[Inferred, Medium-High] Why this one, and not the FPV doctrine.** Acoustic
detection transfers because it is **cheap, passive, needs no spectrum authority,
needs no trained pilot, and its value scales with the number of nodes rather than
with the skill of the operator.** The FPV kill chain does not transfer because its
scarce input is *thousands of trained crews flying every day*, which is a wartime
social fact rather than a purchasable capability. **The thing that travels is the
sensor, not the shooter.** That is the most useful transfer heuristic in this
document, and section 12 builds on it.

### The component chokepoint is Chinese, and everyone knows it [Reported, Medium-High — added on the second pass]

- China restricted exports of drone components — flight controllers, carbon
  frames, motors, radio modules, navigation cameras — to Ukraine and Russia
  from September 2024, and has reduced sales of batteries, motors and controllers
  to the US, Europe and Ukraine while transfers to Russia reportedly continued.
  Skydio is reported to have rationed batteries into spring 2025.
- Nearly **97% of Ukrainian producers** name China as a primary import source;
  about **89% of Ukraine's UAS-related imports by value** came from China in
  H1 2024.
- **March 2026: Ukraine reported its first drone built with zero Chinese
  components**, including domestically produced circuit boards. Mass production
  at that standard is assessed as **years away**.
- Europe's workaround is Taiwan: **Taiwanese drone exports up 749%
  year-on-year** in a March 2026 report, with exports to Czechia rising from
  negligible to about **20,000/month** in October 2025 and about **40,000/month**
  by January 2026. China is reported to be working to sever that link.

*[Inferred, Medium] For a game this is a build-cost mechanic, not background.*
**Cheap drones are cheap because of a supply chain that one state controls.** An
alternative-theatre campaign in which the cheap-airframe economy can be switched
off by an off-map political event models 2026 more honestly than a fixed build
cost, and it is a good campaign-arc lever.

## 5. The Middle East

**One-way attack drones as an offensive US capability.** [Reported/Announced,
High] This is the surprise of 2026. CENTCOM stood up **Scorpion Strike** in
December 2025 as its first one-way attack squadron, and announced **Falcon
Strike** on 13 August 2026 as a multidomain, multinational task force employing
one-way attack systems in the air, on the surface and underwater, led by
SOCCENT with partner nations invited. The United States has stopped treating
the loitering munition as something that happens *to* it.

**Counter-drone against US forces.** [Reported, High] Merops was pushed to the
region to defeat Shahed-pattern drones specifically because it puts a cheap
layer under Patriot and THAAD. The US Air Force has also been publicising
base-defence counter-drone weapons. Reporting during 2026 frames all of this as
lesson-taking from Ukraine and the Middle East simultaneously.

**The cost curve, with numbers.** [Reported, High] Between autumn 2023 and
winter 2025 the US Navy expended **220 anti-air missiles** defending Red Sea
shipping, at a total near **$1bn** — roughly **$4.5m per missile** — against
drones and missiles worth a tiny fraction of that. The replacements:

| Interceptor | Reported unit cost | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Coyote Block 2 | ~$100,000 | |
| Coyote 2C | ~$125,000 | |
| Roadrunner-M | ~$500,000 (est., from a $250m contract) | Recoverable, refuelable, relaunchable |
| Merops interceptor | ~$14,500 | Land-based, Ukraine-derived lineage |
| AIM-9 (as used over Poland) | >€400,000 | |

**What was learned.** [Inferred from the above, Medium-High]

1. *Magazine depth beats interceptor quality.* A ship with 96 cells and $4.5m
   rounds loses to a shed with a hundred airframes.
2. *Layering is mandatory.* Cheap interceptor, then gun, then missile — and get
   the cheap layer to do most of the work.
3. *Recoverability is a cost lever*, hence Roadrunner-M's design.
4. *One-way attack drones are now a US offensive tool*, which means adversary
   counter-drone becomes a US targeting problem too.

### The 2026 Iran war — the largest omission in the first version [Reported, High — added on the second pass]

The first version of this document treated the Middle East as procurement
context. It is not. **From late February 2026 there has been a sustained
state-on-state drone and missile exchange in the Gulf**, still running in
September 2026, and it is the closest published analogue to the kind of fight an
alternative KILL ZONE campaign would model.

**What happened, in outline.**

- Iran began retaliatory strikes on US and Kuwaiti targets in Kuwait from
  **28 February 2026**. On **1 March 2026** an Iranian one-way attack drone struck
  a **US tactical operations centre at Port Shuaiba**, killing **six US Army
  Reserve soldiers** — the first US fatalities of the war. **Air defence failed
  to intercept it.** Camp Buehring was struck the same day; later reporting
  described an Iranian F-5 penetrating Camp Buehring's layered defences and
  completing a bombing run.
- Strikes extended to **Bahrain, Oman (Duqm and Salalah, with a fuel tank
  damaged), Jordan and Iraq**. Iranian strikes and US counter-strikes were still
  being exchanged into **September 2026**.
- **The Strait of Hormuz became a shipping crisis**; flights in and out of the
  region were halted and shipping rerouted.

| Iranian Shahed production | **>200/month** from Iranian facilities alone, plus the jet-powered **Shahed-238** | [Reported, Low-Medium — single framing] |

**[Inferred, High] What this proves that Ukraine does not.** Ukraine shows what
happens when a defender has cheap interceptors, deep sensor coverage and a
domestic production base. **The Gulf shows what happens when a defender has an
excellent, expensive, shallow magazine and no cheap layer.** The defence mostly
worked and was still strategically defeated, because it spent two thirds of a
global stockpile doing it and cannot restock for years. Gulf states publicly
worried the shortage would degrade their own defence, and restocking is described
as something Russia and China are watching.

**[Inferred, Medium-High] Two consequences the game should take literally.**

1. **Interceptor inventory must be a campaign resource with a slow, off-map
   refill.** Not a per-mission ammo bar. Winning every engagement and ending the
   campaign with an empty magazine should be a losing outcome, and it should be
   the *default* outcome for a player who engages everything.
2. **A leaker is not a failure state, it is the normal case.** A ~90-93% defence
   still means a drone reaches the tactical operations centre and kills six
   people. Design the consequence of the one that gets through, not the
   percentage.

**Ukrainian counter-drone technology went to the Gulf, not the other way.**
[Reported, Medium] Merops deployed to the region against Shahed-pattern drones;
Sky Fortress acoustic detection was fielded at **Prince Sultan Air Base** in April
2026; and the transfer of Ukrainian counter-drone experience to Gulf states and
CENTCOM is described as the most significant counter-proliferation dynamic of the
2026 conflict. **Four years of Ukrainian Shahed defence is the most exportable
body of knowledge in the war** — more so than anything about FPVs.

**Gulf procurement followed immediately.** [Reported, High] March 2026: US
approval of ~**$8.5bn** in potential UAE sales, including **$2.1bn of counter-UAS**
and a **$4.5bn** long-range discrimination radar for THAAD integration. **21,500
APKWS II rockets** approved for Israel, Qatar and the UAE for counter-drone and
counter-missile use — a $20-30k guided rocket in place of a multi-million-dollar
missile is the same cost-exchange logic as Merops, applied to an existing
airframe. Counter-UAS is described as **the single fastest-growing defence
procurement category in the GCC**. The US Army issued an RFI in February 2026 for
the **Red Sands Hard Kill Challenge** in Saudi Arabia.

## 6. Other conflicts and non-state actors

[Reported, High] **Sudan** is the clearest case of long-range one-way attack
capability in the hands of a non-state actor. The RSF has struck Port Sudan,
Atbara, Dongola, Khartoum airport and other targets far behind the front, using
long-range delta-wing one-way attack drones assessed by Janes and PAX as being
in roughly the **2,000 km** class. A January 2026 RSF drone attack in southeast
Sudan was reported to have killed 27. The SAF moved its Bayraktars underground.

[Reported, High] **Cartels.** By 2024 there were reports of Mexican and
Colombian cartel personnel training on FPV with the Ukrainian International
Legion. Larger cartels modify commercial airframes and use FPV for one-way
attacks where precision matters. Academic and Small Wars Journal treatments in
2026 describe this as an established operational pattern, not an anomaly.

[Reported, Medium] **Myanmar.** In March 2026, six Ukrainian nationals and one
American were arrested in India's Mizoram state, accused of smuggling European
drones to Myanmar resistance factions.

[Reported, Medium-High] **The Sahel.** Insurgent groups expanded scale and
sophistication through the first half of 2026, reaching Bamako and Niamey and
targeting critical infrastructure. FPV one-way attack, munition-drop and spotter
drones are described as having spread from Syria, Lebanon and Palestine to Haiti
and Nigeria.

[Inferred, Medium] The transmission mechanism is *people and video*, not
smuggled hardware. Airframes are commercial. What propagates is the tactic
library. Expect fibre-optic control and comms-free terminal guidance to reach
non-state hands within a year or two of becoming standard in Ukraine.

### That prediction already came true, faster than stated [Reported, Medium-High — added on the second pass]

**Hezbollah.** Since **March 2026**, fibre-optic FPV drones have been the
prominent weapon used against IDF forces in southern Lebanon. In one reported
period **80+ explosive drones were launched, about 15 hit**, killing four
soldiers and a civilian and injuring dozens. Israel assesses the drones are made
locally and are easy to produce: an off-the-shelf airframe, a small explosive
charge, and transparent wire available on the consumer market.

**That ~19% hit rate is the most useful number in this section.** [Inferred,
Medium] It is an unjammable control link in the hands of a competent but
non-industrial user, against an alert modern army, and it lands roughly one shot
in five. Ukrainian FPV hit rates in clean conditions are quoted at 30-60%. **The
gap between those two numbers is the value of trained crews and a mature kill
chain, isolated from the hardware.** If the game wants a single lever for
"experience", this is its empirical size.

**The fibre supply chain is Chinese and civilian.** [Reported, Medium] China is
described as supplying the whole ecosystem — raw fibre, preform material,
specialised reels, lightweight and Kevlar-reinforced cables, winding machinery,
test equipment and small-scale commercial sales channels — which is what allowed
Russian fibre-FPV production to scale and what puts the same capability in
Hezbollah's hands.

**Fibre is no longer invulnerable.** [Reported, Medium] In **January 2026 Epirus
demonstrated the first directed-energy kill of a fibre-optic-controlled drone**
with its Leonidas high-power microwave system. The same system is reported
defeating **49 of 49** and **61 of 61** drones in live-fire swarm demonstrations,
and a self-driving **Leonidas AGV** variant was unveiled in March 2026 with
General Dynamics Land Systems and Kodiak AI. **A fibre link defeats jamming; it
does not defeat a weapon that cooks the airframe's electronics regardless of how
it is controlled.** For the game's signature model this matters: an RF signature
of 0 should confer immunity to *RF-cued detection and jamming*, not to a
point-defence weapon that does not care about the link.

**Satellite connectivity is a revocable resource, and 2026 revoked some of it.**
[Reported, Medium-High] Starlink is not officially available in Sudan, yet the
RSF uses terminals across all active fronts — obtained through UAE black markets
— to guide drones and coordinate operations, and Sudan's government complained
formally to SpaceX. In Myanmar, terminals are unauthorised but relied on by
resistance forces to fly drones and evacuate wounded; **from 17 August 2026
SpaceX began enforcing a 30-day limit on terminals used outside their country of
registration**, and terminals have been blocked.

*[Inferred, Medium-High]* **Satellite backhaul is the thing most likely to be
switched off mid-campaign, and it is switched off by a company, not by an enemy.**
A game that treats satellite link as terrain rather than as a granted, revocable
service is modelling 2022, not 2026. This is a strong campaign-arc mechanic and
nobody else uses it.

- The US Army moved its counter-drone experimentation programme **from Europe to

### What China is fielding [Reported, Medium]

- **Layered, and heavily directed-energy.** The **Guangjian-11E** and
  **Guangjian-21A** mobile laser systems are shown engaging quadcopters and

*[Inferred, Medium]* **China is building the counter-drone force that Ukraine
never had access to**, because it can afford directed energy and has no shortage
of power generation on a ship. **A Pacific campaign should not reuse the game's
Ukraine-derived point-defence model.** See section 13.

### The geography, restated [Inferred, Medium {D} upgraded from Low-Medium]

The first version's reasoning holds and is now partly corroborated by the
exercise evidence above.

- **Water instead of land.** No contact line to saturate with FPV. The
  short-range quadcopter has no equivalent role across the strait until a landing
  is under way {D} at which point it has an enormous one, against boats and
  beachheads. Taiwan's own procurement backs this: **mass small drones plus 1,600
  USVs**, not a trench-line FPV force.
- **Range is the binding constraint.** Fixed-wing, long-endurance and maritime
  surface/subsurface systems over multirotors. Corroborated by the US Navy's
  FY2026 investment of nearly **$7bn** in autonomous systems and a 2027 inventory
  projection of ~**11 medium USVs and ~500 small USVs**.
- **Dispersal is the defence**, and RIMPAC 2026 is explicitly built around that
  proposition.
- **Magazine depth is worse, not better.** Resupply across the Pacific is a
  months-long problem. The Iran war's Patriot depletion (section 5) is the
  strongest available evidence that this is the real failure mode.
- **Sensing is radar- and space-dominated.** No terrain masking over water, no
  forest to hide a launch team in, acoustic largely useless at sea state. Passive
  RF and radar carry the load {D} though note that Sky Fortress-style acoustic
  detection is being advocated *for* INDOPACOM, presumably for island and base
  defence rather than open water.

*Still not verified, and worth a further pass:* actual delivered quantities
against Taiwan's announced orders; PLA drone production scale; whether Taiwan's
NT$240bn restores the deleted domestic procurement or replaces it with something
different; and any published counter-drone performance data from Chinese systems
beyond demonstration claims.

## 8. A Ukraine-front fight versus a NATO-peer fight

This is the section that matters most for an alternative campaign. Everything
here is **[Inferred, Medium]** unless tagged otherwise — it is reasoning from
the sourced material above, not a sourced claim in itself.

| Dimension | Ukraine front (what the game models) | NATO vs peer |
|---|---|---|
| **Force density** | Extreme along a narrow band. Sensors and shooters overlap continuously; the "kill zone" is 10–20 km deep and effectively total. | Thin over vast area. Point defence at bases, cities, ports. Large gaps between defended points. Detection becomes a *search* problem, not a saturation problem. |
| **Air power** | Effectively absent over the contact line; neither side flies manned aircraft into contested airspace. This is *why* drones dominate. | Present and decisive. AWACS, tankers, fifth-gen fighters; airborne radar sees hundreds of kilometres. A drone's survival time is short — but each kill costs €400k+ [Reported]. |
| **Standoff weapons** | Short-range dominance; deep strike is drone-and-missile, slow, attritional. | Deep strike from the first hour, in volume, at range. Launch sites, factories and logistics are targets on night one, not month six. |
| **Satellite access** | Contested but available; commercial constellations are a lifeline and a vulnerability. | Assured for NATO, contested for the adversary. Persistent ISR, GPS-guided everything, over-the-horizon comms as a baseline rather than a prize. |
| **Electronic warfare** | Pervasive but local — jamming bubbles a few kilometres wide, which is why fibre-optic control matters so much. | Theatre-scale. Wideband denial over hundreds of kilometres, GNSS spoofing as a regional condition rather than a local one. The RF channel largely stops working for *everyone*. |
| **Tempo** | Grinding. Months of positional attrition; innovation cycles of weeks. | Compressed. The decisive phase is days. There is no time for the Ukrainian innovation loop — you fight with the stock on hand. |
| **Political envelope** | Total war for one side; few targeting constraints. | Ambiguity and attribution dominate. Russian, Ukrainian, or lost? [Reported: Ukrainian-origin drones entered Baltic airspace in March and May 2026.] ROE over your own population is a real constraint. |
| **Economics** | Both sides mass-produce cheaply; exchange ratios roughly symmetric. | Grossly asymmetric *against* the defender unless the cheap interceptor layer works — the one point where NATO has actually changed behaviour [Reported]. |

**The synthesis.** [Inferred, Medium] A NATO-peer fight is not "Ukraine with
better equipment." It is a fight where the defender can see and reach much
further but cannot afford to shoot, and where the attacker's problem is not
surviving the kill zone but *getting through a thin, expensive net across a
huge area before the political system decides what the intrusion means.*

The Ukraine front is a **saturation** problem. The NATO flank is a **coverage
and cost** problem. Those are different games.

*Second-pass note:* this table still stands, but it is not the only alternative.
**Section 12** sets out which practices actually transferred between theatres and
which did not, and **section 13** names the specific places where the game's
current model is fitted to Ukraine alone. The 2026 Iran war (section 5) is a
third shape again: not saturation and not coverage, but **magazine depth** — an
excellent defence losing because it runs out.

## 9. Procurement: direction and pace

[Reported, High] **Where the money is going:** cheap kinetic interceptors
(Merops, Coyote, Roadrunner), radar integration so existing sensors can cue
them, base defence, and — newly — offensive one-way attack capability for
Western forces themselves (Scorpion Strike, Falcon Strike). Plus buying
Ukrainian IP rather than Ukrainian product.

[Reported, High] **How fast, where it worked:** Merops went from requirement to
fielded-and-trained in two allied countries inside roughly two months of the
September 2025 incursion, using an accelerated US Army acquisition route
(G-TEAD). That is the benchmark.

[Reported/Announced] **How fast, where it did not:** the EU drone wall was
promised around September 2025, was publicly noted as non-existent in July
2026, has a Baltic funding request rejected in March 2026 without published
reason, and carries completion dates that vary between end-2027 and end-2028
depending on the statement.

[Inferred, Medium] **Is it fast enough? No.** The honest assessment:

- Incursion rates rose roughly tenfold between the pre-September-2025 period and
  the months after [Reported]. Delivered counter-drone coverage did not.
- Two months from incident to fielded system is genuinely fast by defence
  standards, and still slower than an adversary iteration cycle that Ukraine's
  experience puts at weeks.
- Nothing yet exists at the scale implied by "wall." What exists is a
  demonstration at a few sites.

- **JIATF-401**, the Pentagon's counter-drone task force, runs a procurement

- The **SAFER SKIES Act** granted US state, local, tribal and territorial law

- **Directed energy is the divergence.** Epirus's Leonidas high-power microwave

  (Guangjian-11E/21A) against 50-80 m targets. Counter-drone is described as the
  "killer app" for directed energy. **Ukraine does not have this. Everyone else is
  buying it.**

**[Inferred, Medium-High] What the money says about 2027-28.**

1. **The winning bet is a cheap interceptor plus a software C2 layer**, not a new
   sensor. Anduril's contract is sold on Lattice fusing drones, radars and
   satellites into one picture; Merops sells on cost per shot. **The scarce thing
   being purchased is decision speed at low cost per engagement.**
2. **Directed energy arrives at scale in 2027-28**, first on ships and fixed
   bases, where power is free. It changes the economics completely: unlimited
   magazine, near-zero cost per shot, short range, bad weather performance, and it
   does not care about the control link.
3. **Interceptor drones become the mid-layer everywhere.** Japan fielding by 2027;
   Merops already in Poland, Romania and the Middle East; interceptor share of
   Ukrainian Shahed kills doubling in four months.
4. **Nobody is buying mass FPV at Ukrainian scale.** The US Army's SkyFoundry
   target is **10,000 drones/month by late 2026** and a million a year by 2028,
   against Ukraine's planned **7 million in 2026** and Russia's **1.5m+ fielded
   small drones**. That is roughly a **70:1** gap in annual combat drone output

## 11. What an alternative NATO-flank campaign would need to change

Concrete implications for KILL ZONE's current model.

**1. Add an interceptor-drone unit class, and make it the cheap answer.** A
Merops analogue — truck-launched, small, fast (280 km/h reported), short range
(5–20 km reported), with a *multi-mode terminal seeker* that homes on RF, radar
return or thermal. This matters for the game's channel model: it is the first
unit that uses the detection channels *as a weapon*, not just as vision. A
fibre-optic quad with RF signature 0 should be much harder for it to finish.

*Second-pass amendment [Reported, Medium]:* **do not let RF=0 mean invulnerable.**
Epirus demonstrated a high-power microwave kill on a fibre-optic drone in January
2026. Model two distinct defeat mechanisms: **seeker-based interception**, which a
fibre quad genuinely degrades because the seeker has no RF to home on, and
**area electromagnetic effects** (HPM), which do not care about the control link
at all. Directed energy should arrive as a late, expensive, short-ranged,
power-hungry point-defence unit with an effectively unlimited magazine — the
correct 2027-28 counter to the fibre quad's current dominance.

**2. Make cost-exchange a resource mechanic, not flavour.** €400,000 Sidewinder
versus $14,500 interceptor versus $2,000 drone is the central tension of the
theatre. A player who can shoot down everything but goes bankrupt doing it is a
correctly modelled 2025–26.

**3. Invert the density assumption.** The sensor table implicitly assumes
coverage. On a flank map the design problem is *gaps* — long unwatched
approaches punctuated by very strong point defence — which makes sensor
placement the player's scarcest decision.

**4. Add airborne early warning with enormous radar reach.** NATO has AWACS and
fighter radar; the Ukraine front does not. This breaks the current reach table —
several thousand map metres of radar, mobile, expensive, and a priority target.
It makes low-altitude terrain-masked approach the only viable ingress.

**5. Theatre-scale EW should switch the RF channel off entirely.** In a peer
fight jamming is a map-wide condition, not a local bubble. When it is on,
fibre-optic and inertial-navigating units are the only things that work, which
promotes the existing RF=0 fibre quad from niche to dominant. Good campaign-arc
mechanic.

**6. Acoustic should be a buildable network — and it is stronger elsewhere than
this document first claimed.** *Corrected on the second pass.* The first version
said the dense Ukrainian acoustic net "has no flank equivalent yet." It does:
**Lithuania is deploying Sky Fortress in 2026**, NATO has funded **15,000**
third-generation sensors, the US fielded the platform at **Prince Sultan Air
Base** in April 2026, and there is published advocacy for the Eastern Flank
Deterrence Line and INDOPACOM [Reported, Medium-High]. The *physics* argument
still holds — acoustic is short-ranged, useless over water at sea state, and
poor against high or fast targets — but the *availability* argument was wrong.
Acoustic is the **cheapest thing a player can build to convert a gap into
coverage**, and it is the one Ukrainian capability that demonstrably exports.
Make it a network the player lays down node by node, keep reach per node short
and reliability low (the game's current acoustic reliability of 52 is about
right), and make its value come from **count**, not from range.

**7. Change the tempo and the win condition.** Ukraine-front missions are
attritional. Flank missions should be **short, ambiguous and political**: a
handful of contacts, a compressed decision window, and consequences for shooting
at the wrong thing. Ukrainian-origin drones entered NATO airspace in March and
May 2026 [Reported] — misidentification is not a contrivance, it is the
documented normal case.

**8. Depth, not frontage.** Targets are ports, power stations, airfields and
rail 300 km behind the line — the RSF's Port Sudan campaign and Russia's
strike pattern both show this shape. A campaign map that is 600 km deep and
lightly held plays completely differently from one that is 20 km deep and
saturated.

**9. If the campaign goes to the Pacific, the model to change is point defence,
not the map.** *Updated on the second pass: section 7 has been re-researched and
is now usable.* Taiwan's shape is **mass small drones plus 1,600 USVs plus a
political system that can delete the budget**, and the opposing counter-drone
force is **directed energy and layered naval terminal defence**, which Ukraine
has never faced. Do not reuse the Ukraine-derived point-defence model there. See
section 13, items 2 and 11.

**10. Added on the second pass: build the Gulf campaign before the flank

## 12. What transferred, what did not, and why

*Added on the second pass. This is the section the brief asked for and the first
version had no budget to write.* **A tactic that works in one theatre and fails
in another tells you what the underlying constraint really was.** That is the
thing a game should model. Everything in this section is **[Inferred, Medium]**
from the sourced material above unless marked otherwise.

### Transferred, and fast

| Practice | Evidence it transferred | The constraint it actually needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Long-range one-way attack drones** | RSF ~2,000 km class in Sudan; Iran across the Gulf; CENTCOM's own Scorpion Strike and Falcon Strike; Russian strikes into NATO airspace | **Money and a workshop.** No contact line, no spectrum control, no trained pilot per shot. This is why it went everywhere first. |
| **Fibre-optic control** | Hezbollah's prominent weapon in south Lebanon from March 2026, locally made from consumer wire [Reported] | **A Chinese civilian supply chain.** Nothing else. That is why it took about a year, not a decade. |
| **Acoustic detection networks** | Lithuania 2026; NATO funds 15,000 sensors; US at Prince Sultan AB April 2026; advocacy for INDOPACOM [Reported] | **Node count and cheap compute.** Passive, no spectrum authority, no operator skill. The most exportable thing in the war. |
| **Cheap interceptors as a layer under expensive SAMs** | Merops to Poland, Romania and the Middle East; Coyote/Roadrunner at sea; Japan fielding interceptor drones by 2027; APKWS II to Israel, Qatar, UAE [Reported] | **An accepted cost-exchange argument.** The Red Sea and then the Iran war supplied it. |
| **Unmanned surface vessels** | Taiwan's 1,600 USVs and the Endeavor Manta explicitly copied from Ukraine's Black Sea designs; Houthi USVs; US Navy ~$7bn FY26 autonomy [Reported] | **A confined sea and a valuable target.** Travels wherever both exist. |
| **Counter-drone C2 software** | Anduril/Lattice as the spine of a $20bn contract; JIATF-401 marketplace joined by Poland, Australia, South Korea [Reported] | **Nothing physical.** Software transfers at software speed. |

### Did not transfer, and probably cannot

| Practice | Where it failed or was absent | The constraint that was really doing the work |
|---|---|---|
| **The trench-line FPV duel** | Nowhere reproduced at comparable density [High] | **Two industrialised armies dug in fifteen metres apart, plus thousands of trained crews flying daily.** Neither is purchasable. |
| **Drone mass** | US target 10,000/month vs Ukraine's ~200,000/month and 7m planned for 2026 | **Wartime industrial mobilisation and a decentralised tech ecosystem.** Analysts explicitly say Western procurement has not replicated the *agility*, not the hardware. |
| **The weeks-long innovation loop** | "Systemic learning deficit" in Western institutions; "everyone is learning the wrong lessons" [Reported analysis] | **Front-line units with authority to change the product.** An institutional fact, not a technical one. |
| **FPV in jungle and mountain** | Canopy kills range and signal; GPS unreliable; thin air cuts lift and endurance; valleys restrict flight paths [Reported analysis] | **Open, compressed, line-of-sight terrain.** Eastern Ukraine's geography was doing more work than anyone credited. |
| **Manned aviation being useless** | TB2s are still flying 1,200+ sorties in Mali, in service with 10+ African states, doing armed reconnaissance and close air support [Reported] {D} while in Ukraine they were withdrawn after heavy EW losses, with flights down to 30 minutes against a 27-hour design endurance | **A peer air defence and EW environment.** Take that away and the MALE drone is not obsolete at all; it is excellent. |
| **The acoustic net over water** | No evidence of maritime acoustic drone detection anywhere | **Ground, and a quiet-ish background.** |

### The three constraints that explain nearly all of it

1. **Density of trained crews.** Ukraine's FPV advantage is people, not airframes.
   Hezbollah with the same unjammable hardware lands about **one shot in five**
   [Reported]; Ukrainian operators in clean conditions are quoted at **30-60%**.
   **That gap is the value of the crew, isolated.**
2. **Terrain and distance.** Compressed, open, line-of-sight ground makes short-
   range drones decisive. Jungle, mountain, and open sea each break a different
   part of the chain {D} signal, endurance, or the very idea of a contact line.
3. **The presence of a peer electromagnetic environment.** Everything that looks
   obsolete in Ukraine (MALE drones, GPS guidance, radio control) is still
   effective where the enemy has no EW. **Obsolescence in this war is
   environment-specific and does not generalise.**

### What that means for the game, in one line

**Model the constraint, not the tactic.** A unit should be strong or weak because
of *crew density, terrain, and electromagnetic environment* {D} three campaign
parameters {D} not because it is "an FPV." If those three dials are exposed, the
same unit roster plays as Ukraine at one setting, the Gulf at another, the Sahel
at a third, and a strait at a fourth. That is a far better use of the research
than building a separate roster per theatre.

## 13. Where KILL ZONE is overfitted to a single conflict

*Added on the second pass, and the most useful thing this document can say.*
Everything here is **[Inferred]** with the confidence marked; each item names the
specific model assumption at risk.

**1. Sensor density is assumed, and it is a Ukraine-only condition. [High]**
The five-channel reach table implicitly describes a battlefield where coverage is
continuous. Nowhere else has that, and nowhere else can afford it {D} the drone
production gap between Ukraine and the US is roughly **70:1**. *Fix:* make sensor
coverage a **built, paid-for, gappy thing** rather than an ambient property of
units. On any non-Ukraine map, the default state should be *unwatched*.

**2. The point-defence model was researched from a conflict that has almost no
directed energy. [High]** Ukraine's counter-drone is guns, EW, missiles and
interceptor drones. The rest of the world in 2026 is buying **high-power
microwave and lasers** {D} Epirus Leonidas killing 49/49 and 61/61 in swarm
demonstrations and, critically, **killing a fibre-optic drone**; China fielding
mobile lasers against targets at 50-80 m. *Fix:* directed energy needs to exist
as a unit class with **unlimited magazine, near-zero cost per shot, short reach,
weather sensitivity and a power requirement**, and it must bypass the RF channel
entirely. Without it, the game's fibre quad is permanently dominant for reasons
that stopped being true in January 2026.

**3. Magazine depth is modelled per mission; the world now models it per war.
[High]** The Iran war consumed **65-80% of the US Patriot stockpile** in about
five months. A defence that wins every engagement and empties the theatre
magazine has lost. *Fix:* interceptors of every tier should draw on a
**campaign-level pool with slow off-map replenishment**, and the cheap layer
should exist precisely to protect that pool.

**4. "Detection equals engagement" is a saturation-war assumption. [Medium-High]**
On the flank, the binding constraints are **legal authority, attribution and
political consequence**, not sensor reach. Airports were closed by drones nobody
could attribute; Ukrainian-origin drones entered Baltic airspace in March and May
2026; Russian EW in Kaliningrad is assessed as *redirecting* drones into NATO
airspace. *Fix:* a **classify-and-authorise step** between detection and
engagement, with a cost for getting it wrong. This is a mechanic, not a message.

**5. The RF channel is modelled as a local bubble. [Medium-High]** Theatre-scale
GNSS denial is already a standing condition over the Baltic and, in August 2026,
across Poland {D} affecting phones, ships and civil aviation, not just drones.
*Fix:* GNSS denial as a **map-wide, persistent, always-on campaign parameter**
with an independent dial from tactical jamming.

**6. Satellite connectivity is treated as terrain. It is a granted service.
[Medium-High]** Starlink's roaming enforcement from **17 August 2026** cut
terminals used outside their country of registration; terminals are blocked in
Myanmar, where resistance forces need them to fly drones. *Fix:* satellite link
as a **revocable resource** that a non-combatant actor can switch off mid-campaign.
Nobody else models this and it is free drama.

**7. The build cost of a cheap drone assumes an open Chinese supply chain.
[Medium]** 97% of Ukrainian producers name China as a primary source; export
controls have bitten; Ukraine's first China-free drone was March 2026 and mass
production at that standard is "years away"; Europe's workaround runs through
Taiwan. *Fix:* a **supply-chain modifier on cheap-unit build cost** that an
off-map event can change.

**8. Acoustic was about to be weakened on a false premise. [Medium]** See
section 11, item 6. The physics argument was right; the availability argument was
wrong. **Acoustic is the cheapest coverage a player can buy and the Ukrainian
technology that most demonstrably exports.**

**9. The game may be pricing the wrong thing as the upgrade. [Medium]** The
recurring 2026 analytical finding is that **the transferable advantage is the
adaptation loop, not the drone.** A tech tree that sells better airframes models
the thing every analyst says is the wrong lesson. *Consider:* an upgrade
currency that buys **iteration speed** {D} how fast a counter to the enemy's last
counter becomes available {D} rather than flat stat increases.

**10. Manned aviation is written out, and that is a Ukraine-specific fact.
[Medium-High]** TB2s were withdrawn in Ukraine after heavy EW losses and are
simultaneously flying **1,200+ sorties in Mali** for at least ten African
operators. Section 8's NATO-peer column already assumes airpower is decisive.
*Fix:* make the airpower and MALE-drone tiers **switchable by campaign
parameter**, because their effectiveness is a property of the enemy's EW, not of
the airframe.

**11. There is no "second war" in the model, and there should be. [High]** The
2026 Iran war is a real, current, state-on-state drone and missile campaign with
published intercept counts, a leaker that killed six people, and a magazine
crisis. **If KILL ZONE builds only one alternative campaign, build that one, not
the NATO flank** {D} the flank is ambiguity and politics, which is a harder game
to make fun; the Gulf is the cost-exchange problem in its purest, most
mechanically legible form.

**What is *not* overfitted, and should be kept.** [Medium-High] The sensor
physics travel unchanged {D} the square-root and fourth-root reach scaling, the
day/night thermal swing, the altitude modifiers, the per-channel reliabilities.
Nothing found in this pass contradicts any of them. **It is the density, the
tempo, the magazine, the authority to shoot and the supply chain that do not
travel.**
