# Beyond Ukraine: how the drone war spread, 2025–26

Research note for KILL ZONE. Compiled 14 September 2026.

**What this is for.** The game's five-channel detection model was built around a
Ukraine-style front: two armies pressed against each other along a narrow
contact line, saturated with cheap sensors and cheap munitions. This document
asks what changes if an alternative campaign is set somewhere else — NATO's
eastern flank, the Gulf, the first island chain. The short answer is that the
*sensor physics* travel unchanged but almost everything around them — density,
tempo, who owns the sky, what a "turn" costs — does not.

**How to read the confidence markers.** Every substantive claim is tagged:

- **[Reported]** — a specific event or figure carried by named reporting.
- **[Announced]** — a government or alliance said it will do something. Not the
  same as it existing. This distinction is doing a lot of work in this document.
- **[Inferred]** — my reasoning from the above. Not sourced.

Confidence is **[High] / [Medium] / [Low]**.

**Research limits, stated up front.** The search budget ran out partway through
and direct page fetches were blocked by network policy, so all content is as
summarised in search results rather than read in full. **Section 7 (Taiwan and
the Pacific) is the weak one** and is flagged in place. No citations have been
invented to fill gaps.

## 1. Summary

**The drone war spread as two different things, not one.** [High] What reached
NATO's eastern flank is the *long-range one-way attack drone* and the political
problem of what to do about it. What reached the Middle East, Africa and Latin
America is that plus the *cheap tactical quadcopter*. The trench-line FPV duel
that defines the Ukraine front has not been reproduced anywhere at comparable
density, because nowhere else has two industrialised armies dug in fifteen
metres apart.

**NATO's eastern flank is being probed, not attacked.** [Reported, High]
Romania recorded 33 airspace incursions since 2022, 18 of them in 2026 alone as
of August. One analysis tracked 30 suspected or confirmed violations between
September 2025 and January 2026, against 23 in the whole preceding period from
March 2022 to August 2025. The rate has stepped up by roughly an order of
magnitude. Nobody has been killed by one on NATO soil in a way that forced
Article 5.

**The alliance's real answer so far is a truck with small interceptor drones on
it, not a wall.** [Reported, High] Merops — built by the US firm Perennial
Autonomy, formerly Project Eagle — went to Poland and Romania from November
2025, at roughly $14,500 per interceptor, against Sidewinders at over €400,000
a shot. That is the single most consequential fact in this document, and the
game should treat it as a unit type, not a detail.

**The "drone wall" is mostly an announcement.** [Announced / High confidence
that it is an announcement] The EU's European Drone Defence Initiative and the
Finland/Poland-led Eastern Flank Watch have timelines (initial capacity end
2026, completion end 2027 per the EU defence commissioner; other framings say
full function end 2028) and funding instruments (SAFE loans), and as of July
2026 critics were publicly noting that ten months after it was promised it did
not exist. A Baltic consortium's funding request was rejected in March 2026
without published reason.

**Ukraine is now an exporter of doctrine and IP, on deliberately asymmetric
terms.** [Reported, High] Under the "Drone Deal" framework Ukraine supplies the
intellectual property and combat experience; partners supply capital and
factory floor. Agreements exist with the Netherlands, Lithuania, Latvia,
Denmark, Estonia and Norway. In July 2026 Ukraine cut its own transfer approval
process to 30 days, and signed a first statement of intent with the United
States.

**The cost-exchange problem is the organising fact of the whole thing.** [High]
Roughly $1bn of US naval interceptors were burned in the Red Sea at about
$4.5m per missile average against targets worth thousands. Every procurement
decision since is downstream of that number.

**Proliferation is effectively unstoppable at the low end.** [High] The RSF is
flying long-range one-way attack drones with something in the 2,000 km class
across Sudan. Cartels, Sahelian insurgents and Myanmar resistance factions all
field FPV. The skill, not the hardware, is the constraint — and the skill is
being taught.

**Is Western procurement fast enough? No, and not close.** [Inferred, Medium]
What arrived quickly arrived because single states used unusual acquisition
authorities. Everything routed through multinational instruments is measured in
years against a threat that iterates in weeks.

## 3. NATO's eastern flank

**The incursions.** [Reported, High] Poland and Romania take the bulk. The
pattern is spillover from strikes on Ukraine rather than deliberate attack —
navigation drift, deliberate routing through NATO airspace to complicate
intercepts, and probing. Romania's 33-since-2022 / 18-in-2026 split is the
clearest published measure of the acceleration. NATO's own language has shifted
from "violations" to Russia demonstrating "increasing tolerance for risk," which
is diplomatic phrasing for *deliberate*.

**What was deployed.** [Reported, High] Eastern Sentry is modest in hardware —
a few squadrons' worth of fighters, a frigate, helicopters, rotated by
contributors. Its significance is procedural: it made eastern-flank air defence
a collective NATO conduct rather than a national one.

**What was bought, and by whom.** [Reported, High]

- **Merops** (US, Perennial Autonomy). Truck-mounted, launches small interceptor
  drones. Reported performance: roughly 5–20 km range, up to 280 km/h, 2 kg
  fragmentation warhead, terminal homing on RF emissions, radar return **or**
  thermal signature. **~$14,500 per interceptor.** Poland and Romania from
  November 2025; subsequently integrated with various radars; subsequently
  deployed to the Middle East against Shahed-pattern drones.
- **Fighters with air-to-air missiles.** Still the default for anything that has
  to be killed *now*, at €400k+ a shot.
- **National walls.** Poland's border/air-defence programme and Romania's
  buildup draw on EU SAFE long-maturity loans.

**What exists versus what was announced.** [High] This is the load-bearing
distinction for a game designer.

*Exists:* Eastern Sentry's rotational air presence; dozens of Merops systems in
two countries; a trained cadre; an experimentation exercise (Eastern Phoenix 26)
that put ~215 systems in one place to see what works; radar integration work.

*Announced:* the European Drone Defence Initiative / "drone wall"; Eastern Flank
Watch as an integrated air-defence, EW, surveillance and maritime picture along
the whole border; €1.26bn across 57 defence projects; initial capacity "end
2026," completion "end 2027" or "end 2028" depending on the statement. As of
July 2026 the public assessment was that the wall did not exist.

*Inference [Medium]:* what the flank actually has in 2026 is **point defence at
a handful of sites plus alert fighters**, not area coverage. A drone that does
not fly over a defended point is not engaged. That is a very different tactical
picture from Ukraine, where sensor coverage along the contact line is close to
continuous.

## 4. Ukrainian-origin systems adopted elsewhere, and the terms

[Reported, High] The **Drone Deal** framework is explicit about the trade:
Ukraine contributes IP and combat-proven designs; the partner contributes
industrial capacity and capital; production is localised in the partner country
or jointly in Ukraine. Signed or in implementation with the **Netherlands,
Lithuania, Latvia, Denmark, Estonia and Norway**. Denmark's version builds on
the earlier "Danish model" of directly financing Ukrainian manufacturers. Norway
planned a pilot production line for 2026 with scaling thereafter.

[Reported, High] In July 2026 Ukraine cut its transfer approval procedure to
**30 days** — a deliberate move to make itself a fast supplier rather than a
bureaucratic one.

[Reported, High] The **US** relationship is newer and narrower: a statement of
intent, not a production contract. Six firms permitted to export roughly 100
drones each for test and evaluation; one firm reported shipping 2,000 units
under a first export permit. This feeds the Pentagon's "drone dominance"
push — reported as a $1bn programme for 200,000 drones by 2027.

[Inferred, Medium] The interesting term is the *direction of the IP*. Ukraine is
not selling finished units at scale; it is selling the right to build, plus the
tacit knowledge of what fails. That is a different commercial shape from arms
export and it is why the agreements multiplied so fast — they cost the partner
capital rather than needing Ukrainian factory output that Ukraine cannot spare.

## 5. The Middle East

**One-way attack drones as an offensive US capability.** [Reported/Announced,
High] This is the surprise of 2026. CENTCOM stood up **Scorpion Strike** in
December 2025 as its first one-way attack squadron, and announced **Falcon
Strike** on 13 August 2026 as a multidomain, multinational task force employing
one-way attack systems in the air, on the surface and underwater, led by
SOCCENT with partner nations invited. The United States has stopped treating
the loitering munition as something that happens *to* it.

**Counter-drone against US forces.** [Reported, High] Merops was pushed to the
region to defeat Shahed-pattern drones specifically because it puts a cheap
layer under Patriot and THAAD. The US Air Force has also been publicising
base-defence counter-drone weapons. Reporting during 2026 frames all of this as
lesson-taking from Ukraine and the Middle East simultaneously.

**The cost curve, with numbers.** [Reported, High] Between autumn 2023 and
winter 2025 the US Navy expended **220 anti-air missiles** defending Red Sea
shipping, at a total near **$1bn** — roughly **$4.5m per missile** — against
drones and missiles worth a tiny fraction of that. The replacements:

| Interceptor | Reported unit cost | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Coyote Block 2 | ~$100,000 | |
| Coyote 2C | ~$125,000 | |
| Roadrunner-M | ~$500,000 (est., from a $250m contract) | Recoverable, refuelable, relaunchable |
| Merops interceptor | ~$14,500 | Land-based, Ukraine-derived lineage |
| AIM-9 (as used over Poland) | >€400,000 | |

**What was learned.** [Inferred from the above, Medium-High]

1. *Magazine depth beats interceptor quality.* A ship with 96 cells and $4.5m
   rounds loses to a shed with a hundred airframes.
2. *Layering is mandatory.* Cheap interceptor, then gun, then missile — and get
   the cheap layer to do most of the work.
3. *Recoverability is a cost lever*, hence Roadrunner-M's design.
4. *One-way attack drones are now a US offensive tool*, which means adversary
   counter-drone becomes a US targeting problem too.

## 6. Other conflicts and non-state actors

[Reported, High] **Sudan** is the clearest case of long-range one-way attack
capability in the hands of a non-state actor. The RSF has struck Port Sudan,
Atbara, Dongola, Khartoum airport and other targets far behind the front, using
long-range delta-wing one-way attack drones assessed by Janes and PAX as being
in roughly the **2,000 km** class. A January 2026 RSF drone attack in southeast
Sudan was reported to have killed 27. The SAF moved its Bayraktars underground.

[Reported, High] **Cartels.** By 2024 there were reports of Mexican and
Colombian cartel personnel training on FPV with the Ukrainian International
Legion. Larger cartels modify commercial airframes and use FPV for one-way
attacks where precision matters. Academic and Small Wars Journal treatments in
2026 describe this as an established operational pattern, not an anomaly.

[Reported, Medium] **Myanmar.** In March 2026, six Ukrainian nationals and one
American were arrested in India's Mizoram state, accused of smuggling European
drones to Myanmar resistance factions.

[Reported, Medium-High] **The Sahel.** Insurgent groups expanded scale and
sophistication through the first half of 2026, reaching Bamako and Niamey and
targeting critical infrastructure. FPV one-way attack, munition-drop and spotter
drones are described as having spread from Syria, Lebanon and Palestine to Haiti
and Nigeria.

[Inferred, Medium] The transmission mechanism is *people and video*, not
smuggled hardware. Airframes are commercial. What propagates is the tactic
library. Expect fibre-optic control and comms-free terminal guidance to reach
non-state hands within a year or two of becoming standard in Ukraine.

## 7. Taiwan and the Pacific

**Confidence: [Low].** My search budget ran out before I could cover this
properly and fetches were blocked. What follows is one 2026 data point plus
general knowledge to roughly May 2026, and it should be re-researched before
anything is built on it.

[Reported, Medium] The US Army has explicitly moved its counter-drone
experimentation programme from Europe to INDOPACOM — the Eastern Sentry /
Eastern Phoenix experimentation model is being ported to the Pacific.

[Inferred, Low-Medium] The geography inverts almost every assumption from the
European flank:

- **Water instead of land.** There is no contact line to saturate with FPV. The
  short-range quadcopter, the defining weapon of Ukraine, has no equivalent role
  across 130 km of strait until a landing is actually underway — at which point
  it has an enormous one, against boats and beachheads.
- **Range is the binding constraint.** Anything that matters must cross water,
  which favours fixed-wing, long-endurance and maritime surface/subsurface
  unmanned systems over multirotors.
- **Dispersal is the defence.** On an island, hardening and dispersal of
  airfields, launchers and command nodes substitutes for depth.
- **Magazine depth is worse, not better.** Resupply across the Pacific is a
  months-long problem, not a truck-convoy problem. The cost-exchange lesson
  from the Red Sea bites hardest here.
- **Sensing is radar- and space-dominated.** Over water there is no terrain
  masking, no forest to hide a launch team in, and acoustic detection is largely
  useless at sea state. Passive RF and radar carry the load.

*What I could not verify and should be checked:* Taiwan's own drone
procurement volumes and domestic industrial base; what recent Han Kuang
exercises actually demonstrated; the current delivery status of US attritable-
systems programmes; PLA counter-drone fielding.

## 8. A Ukraine-front fight versus a NATO-peer fight

This is the section that matters most for an alternative campaign. Everything
here is **[Inferred, Medium]** unless tagged otherwise — it is reasoning from
the sourced material above, not a sourced claim in itself.

| Dimension | Ukraine front (what the game models) | NATO vs peer |
|---|---|---|
| **Force density** | Extreme along a narrow band. Sensors and shooters overlap continuously; the "kill zone" is 10–20 km deep and effectively total. | Thin over vast area. Point defence at bases, cities, ports. Large gaps between defended points. Detection becomes a *search* problem, not a saturation problem. |
| **Air power** | Effectively absent over the contact line; neither side flies manned aircraft into contested airspace. This is *why* drones dominate. | Present and decisive. AWACS, tankers, fifth-gen fighters; airborne radar sees hundreds of kilometres. A drone's survival time is short — but each kill costs €400k+ [Reported]. |
| **Standoff weapons** | Short-range dominance; deep strike is drone-and-missile, slow, attritional. | Deep strike from the first hour, in volume, at range. Launch sites, factories and logistics are targets on night one, not month six. |
| **Satellite access** | Contested but available; commercial constellations are a lifeline and a vulnerability. | Assured for NATO, contested for the adversary. Persistent ISR, GPS-guided everything, over-the-horizon comms as a baseline rather than a prize. |
| **Electronic warfare** | Pervasive but local — jamming bubbles a few kilometres wide, which is why fibre-optic control matters so much. | Theatre-scale. Wideband denial over hundreds of kilometres, GNSS spoofing as a regional condition rather than a local one. The RF channel largely stops working for *everyone*. |
| **Tempo** | Grinding. Months of positional attrition; innovation cycles of weeks. | Compressed. The decisive phase is days. There is no time for the Ukrainian innovation loop — you fight with the stock on hand. |
| **Political envelope** | Total war for one side; few targeting constraints. | Ambiguity and attribution dominate. Russian, Ukrainian, or lost? [Reported: Ukrainian-origin drones entered Baltic airspace in March and May 2026.] ROE over your own population is a real constraint. |
| **Economics** | Both sides mass-produce cheaply; exchange ratios roughly symmetric. | Grossly asymmetric *against* the defender unless the cheap interceptor layer works — the one point where NATO has actually changed behaviour [Reported]. |

**The synthesis.** [Inferred, Medium] A NATO-peer fight is not "Ukraine with
better equipment." It is a fight where the defender can see and reach much
further but cannot afford to shoot, and where the attacker's problem is not
surviving the kill zone but *getting through a thin, expensive net across a
huge area before the political system decides what the intrusion means.*

The Ukraine front is a **saturation** problem. The NATO flank is a **coverage
and cost** problem. Those are different games.

## 9. Procurement: direction and pace

[Reported, High] **Where the money is going:** cheap kinetic interceptors
(Merops, Coyote, Roadrunner), radar integration so existing sensors can cue
them, base defence, and — newly — offensive one-way attack capability for
Western forces themselves (Scorpion Strike, Falcon Strike). Plus buying
Ukrainian IP rather than Ukrainian product.

[Reported, High] **How fast, where it worked:** Merops went from requirement to
fielded-and-trained in two allied countries inside roughly two months of the
September 2025 incursion, using an accelerated US Army acquisition route
(G-TEAD). That is the benchmark.

[Reported/Announced] **How fast, where it did not:** the EU drone wall was
promised around September 2025, was publicly noted as non-existent in July
2026, has a Baltic funding request rejected in March 2026 without published
reason, and carries completion dates that vary between end-2027 and end-2028
depending on the statement.

[Inferred, Medium] **Is it fast enough? No.** The honest assessment:

- Incursion rates rose roughly tenfold between the pre-September-2025 period and
  the months after [Reported]. Delivered counter-drone coverage did not.
- Two months from incident to fielded system is genuinely fast by defence
  standards, and still slower than an adversary iteration cycle that Ukraine's
  experience puts at weeks.
- Nothing yet exists at the scale implied by "wall." What exists is a
  demonstration at a few sites.

## 10. Proliferation: who can build these, and for how much

[Reported/Inferred, High] The tiers, as of 2026:

1. **Anyone with a hobby shop.** FPV quadcopter, commercial airframe, commercial
   flight controller, improvised warhead. Tens to low hundreds of dollars per
   unit. Demonstrated by cartels, Sahelian insurgents, Myanmar factions.
2. **Any organised armed group with a workshop and a supplier.** Munition-drop
   quadcopters, spotter drones, short-range one-way attack. Low thousands.
3. **Any group with state-adjacent backing.** Long-range delta-wing one-way
   attack in the 1,000–2,000 km class — the RSF has these [Reported]. Tens of
   thousands per unit.
4. **States.** Mass-produced Shahed-class, jet-powered variants, fibre-optic and
   comms-free terminal guidance, layered decoys.

[Inferred, Medium] The interceptor side proliferates too — a $14,500
interceptor is within reach of many countries — and the two converge: an
interceptor with a warhead pointed at the ground is a loitering munition. The
scarce goods are **trained operators**, **a sanctions-proof supply chain**, and
**the tacit knowledge of what breaks**. Ukraine sells the third legally to
allies [Reported]; it leaks illegally to everyone else.

## 11. What an alternative NATO-flank campaign would need to change

Concrete implications for KILL ZONE's current model.

**1. Add an interceptor-drone unit class, and make it the cheap answer.** A
Merops analogue — truck-launched, small, fast (280 km/h reported), short range
(5–20 km reported), with a *multi-mode terminal seeker* that homes on RF, radar
return or thermal. This matters for the game's channel model: it is the first
unit that uses the detection channels *as a weapon*, not just as vision. A
fibre-optic quad with RF signature 0 should be much harder for it to finish.

**2. Make cost-exchange a resource mechanic, not flavour.** €400,000 Sidewinder
versus $14,500 interceptor versus $2,000 drone is the central tension of the
theatre. A player who can shoot down everything but goes bankrupt doing it is a
correctly modelled 2025–26.

**3. Invert the density assumption.** The sensor table implicitly assumes
coverage. On a flank map the design problem is *gaps* — long unwatched
approaches punctuated by very strong point defence — which makes sensor
placement the player's scarcest decision.

**4. Add airborne early warning with enormous radar reach.** NATO has AWACS and
fighter radar; the Ukraine front does not. This breaks the current reach table —
several thousand map metres of radar, mobile, expensive, and a priority target.
It makes low-altitude terrain-masked approach the only viable ingress.

**5. Theatre-scale EW should switch the RF channel off entirely.** In a peer
fight jamming is a map-wide condition, not a local bubble. When it is on,
fibre-optic and inertial-navigating units are the only things that work, which
promotes the existing RF=0 fibre quad from niche to dominant. Good campaign-arc
mechanic.

**6. Acoustic should be weaker, not stronger.** Over water it barely works; over
a thin wide flank there is nobody in the right field to hear anything. The dense
Ukrainian acoustic net has no flank equivalent yet — consider acoustic as a
*buildable network* rather than a unit property.

**7. Change the tempo and the win condition.** Ukraine-front missions are
attritional. Flank missions should be **short, ambiguous and political**: a
handful of contacts, a compressed decision window, and consequences for shooting
at the wrong thing. Ukrainian-origin drones entered NATO airspace in March and
May 2026 [Reported] — misidentification is not a contrivance, it is the
documented normal case.

**8. Depth, not frontage.** Targets are ports, power stations, airfields and
rail 300 km behind the line — the RSF's Port Sudan campaign and Russia's
strike pattern both show this shape. A campaign map that is 600 km deep and
lightly held plays completely differently from one that is 20 km deep and
saturated.

**9. If the campaign goes to the Pacific instead, re-research first.** Section 7
is not solid enough to build on.
