
RADAR - a mast against a vehicle on the ground
Radar Mast at 14,400 m; a Main Tank at its catalogue 7.5 m/s driving a
12,000 m leg past it. The sweep is the vehicle's closest approach and its
heading, because what gates this channel is radial velocity and not range
the mast holds a tank closing head-on out to 8044 m, and a tank crossing its face at 0 m
one run per cell, not sixty: nothing in this engagement draws from a
random stream, so a second trial is the same drive (see the comment)
  world: mixed terrain (road, forest, rubble over open ground), weather Clear, ground Firm, territory split at 21600 m (team 1 west / team 2 east), imagery granted over each side's own ground

  (a) the notch. Each cell is the share of the drive's ticks the mast
  holds the tank at all.

  closest approach   driving in   driving across   parked
  --------------------------------------------------------------
             300 m          46%              29%       0%
            1200 m          47%              31%       0%
            2400 m          53%              39%       0%
            3600 m          50%              79%       0%
            4800 m          28%              49%       0%
            6000 m          13%              26%       0%
            7200 m           0%               0%       0%

  The parked column is the notch in its pure form: zero at every range,
  because a vehicle at rest has no radial component and no cross-section
  and no proximity can buy its way out of a hard gate. That is the one
  cell-for-cell comparison in the table - same position, same tank, same
  mast, nothing different but whether the engine is running.

  The two moving columns are NOT a controlled comparison of heading and
  should not be read as one. Both legs are 12,000 m, but a leg driven at
  the mast spans every range from 13 km down to the stated one while a
  leg driven across its face never leaves the stated one by more than
  half its length, so the crossing column is fought much closer in. That
  is why it overtakes the closing column past about 3 km rather than
  being quieter everywhere: inside the envelope the notch costs the
  crosser most of its contact, and outside it the closer has no contact
  to lose. Both facts are about a real manoeuvre and neither is about
  the other.

  The crossing column peaking at 3,600 m is the sharpest thing here and
  is left standing rather than smoothed. Nearer than that, a crossing
  tank spends most of its leg inside the angle where the radial falls
  into the 1.5-4 m/s band - short reach and 30% reliability, so the
  contact is both closer and intermittent. Further out, the band is
  irrelevant because the whole leg is past what the mast reaches. 3,600 m
  is where the geometry puts the whole crossing inside the medium band
  and inside the envelope at once, and it is the best place on this map
  to be a tank the mast can see.

  (b) emission control - FINDINGS 37's order, issued through
  Command.SetEmitting and never before in a balance table. Two rows and
  not a sweep, and the comment on this method says why.

  the mast is    heard at   a listener at 3,000 m holds it   its own pass
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  radiating        4425 m                   100% of ticks            47%
  dark             2400 m                     0% of ticks             0%

  'heard at' is how far an EW Truck's passive listening reaches the mast,
  read off a spawned truck in a stepped world. 'its own pass' is the
  1,200 m closing drive from (a), so the last column is what the order
  costs and the first two are what it buys.

  Switching off does not hide the mast. It moves the range at which the
  other side hears it from a little over four kilometres to a little
  under two and a half - a factor of about 1.8 - because a quiet mast is
  still a structure with a radio signature of its own. What it pays for
  that is everything: against a ground target a dark mast holds nothing
  at any range on any heading, because its only other sensor is passive
  RF and a Main Tank's radio signature is zero. So the order is not a
  posture a player can hold while still working - it is a decision to
  stop seeing in exchange for halving the radius inside which somebody
  can find you.

  (c) the rest of the ground roster against the same mast, each vehicle
  closing head-on at its own catalogue speed. The band is the Doppler
  band that speed lands in; the reach and the share of the pass are
  measured off a driving vehicle rather than computed from it.

  Four vehicles and not the whole roster, for two reasons that are the
  same reason. The two ground robots are radio-controlled, so without a
  command post they stop of their own accord and a parked robot is the
  notch rather than a measurement. And the EW Truck and the Logistics
  UGV carry radio signatures the mast hears on its ESM, which would make
  the share of the pass a measurement of something other than radar.
  Every vehicle below is crewed and radio-silent, so the only thing that
  can find it here is the channel this experiment is about.

  vehicle              speed   cross-section   band     held out to   the pass
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Motorcycle Squad    17 m/s              64   full           4240 m        33%
  Supply Truck        14 m/s              88   full           8460 m        69%
  IFV                  9 m/s              90   medium         7169 m        55%
  Main Tank          7.5 m/s              94   medium         8044 m        47%

  Speed and size pull in opposite directions on this channel, and the
  roster puts them in opposition. Everything armoured is slow - the tank
  at 7.5 m/s and the IFV at 9 - so both are stuck in the 4-10 m/s band
  whatever they do, a fifth of the mast's reach against them is spent
  before the engagement starts, and no manoeuvre can get it back. The
  only ground units that can be a full-strength return are the two
  soft-skinned ones, and they are also the two with the smallest
  cross-sections.

  Which way that trade lands is not obvious and the table is the only
  place it is answered. For the Supply Truck the band wins: 88 of
  cross-section at full Doppler reaches further than the tank's 94 at
  0.80 of it, so the loudest thing on the ground to a radar mast is a
  supply truck and not a tank. For the Motorcycle Squad it loses badly:
  64 of cross-section is about half the tank's reach even at full
  Doppler, and it crosses what is left at seventeen metres a second.

  So a radar mast is at its best against exactly the traffic it was not
  bought for, and a player who wants to move armour under one has the
  answer in the first table rather than this one: do not drive at it.
