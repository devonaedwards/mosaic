
APERTURE - the same 600 m camera, spread over different arcs
  world: mixed terrain (road, forest, rubble over open ground), weather Clear, ground Firm, territory split at 1100 m (team 1 west / team 2 east), imagery granted over each side's own ground

  The law. Reach scales as the square root of how narrow the arc is;
  this is the formula evaluated, not a fight, and it moves only when a
  signature or an optical constant moves.

  arc      reach vs a quad   covered at once   heads for 360   total cost
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
    30 deg            255 m                8%              12     5400 MAT
    45 deg            208 m               12%               8     3600 MAT
    90 deg            147 m               25%               4     1800 MAT
   120 deg            127 m               33%               3     1350 MAT
   180 deg            104 m               50%               2      900 MAT
   360 deg             73 m              100%               1      450 MAT

  What it is worth. Four mounts differing only in their head, against
  three FPV Teams arriving one every 3 s from the west.

  head                                         reach on axis   mount survives
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  30 deg staring, facing east (default)               255 m              45%
  30 deg staring, turned to face the threat           255 m              71%
  120 deg staring, facing east (default)              127 m              45%
  120 deg sweeping at 70 deg/s (shipped)              127 m              63%
  360 deg, no blind side                               73 m              71%

  "Reach on axis" is what the head finds when it happens to be pointed
  the right way - DetectionRangeFor answers the range question and not
  the bearing one - so rows one and two share a number and not an
  outcome. That is the whole content of an arc.

  The second row is the only one that turns its head, and it does so by
  a direct write to SensorSuite.Facing from this harness, because the
  simulation has no way to aim a staring sensor: World.Spawn sets the
  facing due east and only SweepSensors ever moves it. Every other row
  is a unit a player could actually buy. The gap between rows one and
  two is therefore not a balance figure, it is the size of a missing
  mechanic.

  Microphones and radio listening are exempt from all of this, because
  they are omnidirectional by nature - which is exactly why they are
  the cheap way to know something is out there and the useless way to
  know where it is. It is also why the narrow heads below are not as
  blind as their arc suggests: these mounts carry the Gun Mount's
  200 m array as well, and it does not care which way the camera is
  pointed.

  Worth reading against the law above it: the 30-degree head sees three
  and a half times as far as the 360-degree one, costs twelve times as
  much to cover the same sky, and - when it is pointed the right way,
  which nothing can currently arrange - survives exactly as often. Above
  about 70 m the extra reach buys a mount nothing, because 70 m is where
  it first holds a track and 3.2 s is all it has after that (FINDINGS
  31). The panoramic head is not the compromise position on this slider;
  on these numbers it is the only one worth buying.
