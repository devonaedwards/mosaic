  The two arms are the result, and they are not what this experiment
  was written to measure. A filament dragged down a road parts after
  about 1,500 m whatever the airframe is carrying: Terrain's snag rate
  is 1.5% per real second on a road and zero on open ground, and at
  22 m/s that is a thread with an expected life of a kilometre and a
  half. Over open ground it does not part at all until the spool runs
  out. So the 16,800 m in the catalogue is not the leash on the route
  every other experiment in this file flies - there the thread is gone
  at a tenth of it, and the number on the unit's card is a number the
  player will never see spent.

  That changes what the third liability is. A thread that parts 1,500 m
  from the pad can only be found by traffic within 1,500 m of the pad,
  so on the road arm the found column collapses as soon as the pad is
  further back than that: the cable is a liability only from a pad that
  was already standing in the enemy's lap. On the open arm the thread
  survives the whole flight and the found column reads 95-100% across a
  twelve-kilometre sweep of standoff. That is FINDINGS 39's hand
  measurement - seven threads found from the forward pad and seven from
  the rear one - reproduced as a sweep rather than as two runs, and it
  holds: how often a cable is found is not a function of where you
  launched. What you launched over decides that.

  The bottom row of the open arm is the spool, and it is the only place
  in this table where the catalogue figure binds: 18,000 m of flight on
  16,800 m of thread parts the line 1,200 m short, four fifths of the
  trials never get a thread across the lane at all, and the found column
  falls to 20%. A leash long enough to be irrelevant everywhere else is
  an asset exactly once.

  The 'site held on radio' column is the control and it is what makes
  the fiber column mean anything. The same sortie flown by an FPV Team
  drags no thread, so where the two agree the filament disclosed nothing
  the defence's own sensors had not - a launch site inside the Radar
  Mast's passive listening is held whatever took off from it, and on
  these rungs that boundary sits between 6,600 m and 9,600 m out. Past
  it the control is zero and the fiber column is not, and those three
  rows are the price of the thread with everything else divided out:
  from a pad the enemy could not otherwise find, one vehicle driving
  over a cable hands him the whole launch site.
