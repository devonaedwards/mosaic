
REACH - what limits a mount's output, the barrel or the seeing
'Test Long Mount' (Defs.cs, test-only), four FPV Teams one every 3 s
from 1,200 m. Each sweep varies one of the mount's own numbers and
holds the other fixed - see the comment on this method for why this is
no longer run on the flat control world.
  world: mixed terrain (road, forest, rubble over open ground), weather Clear, ground Firm, territory split at 1550 m (team 1 west / team 2 east), imagery granted over each side's own ground

  (a) the barrel, with the mount's 600 m optics held fixed

  ------------------------------------------------------------

   450 m               20.5 s       2.6              46%
   350 m               15.9 s       2.7              42%
   250 m               11.4 s       2.8              36%
   180 m                8.2 s       2.9              23%
   120 m                5.5 s       3.1              12%
    85 m                3.9 s       3.0              22%   <- the deployed barrel

  world                              arrived   mount survives
  ------------------------------------------------------------
  flat, clear, firm, ownerless           2.7             100%

  That row is the same 550 m mount as the top of (a), on the world every
  experiment ran on before AUDIT F34 was fixed. FINDINGS 33 recorded the
  two worlds as identical in every cell. They are not any more: the flat
  world has no territory owner, so every square metre of it is denied
  ground, every FPV Team's dead reckoning crosses the miss radius on the
  way in, and the warheads land on empty grass. Nothing about the mount
  changed. The world did, and something finally reads it.

  Read the two 'mount survives' columns against each other. They are the
  same mount, the same attack and the same seeds; the only difference is
  which of its two numbers is being spent. Whichever column moves is the
  one that is binding, and that is the number a player's upgrade should
  be buying.

  Both of them move, which is not what the old flat sweep implied and is
  not quite what FINDINGS 31 says either. The barrel matters a great
  deal here and mattered not at all before, and the difference is only
  the spacing: reach buys time, and time is worth nothing against a
  flight that arrives in one instant and everything against one that
  keeps arriving. 'Barrel length barely matters' is true of a wave and
  false of a stream.

  The optics column is not monotonic and that is the more interesting
  half. A mount that finds the drone at 64 m does better than one that
  finds it at 127, because a five-round belt spent at the rim of the
  envelope is a belt spent at the worst hit chance the falloff offers.
  Seeing further is only an advantage to a mount that can afford to
  wait, and this one cannot.
