  A mount is bought to service an engagement, and the rounds column
  is where the money goes. Two things changed with the rescale and
  they are worth separating.

  The supporting mounts now shoot in both arms. In compressed units
  the third and fourth mounts of a cluster fired 0.00 rounds against a
  wave; here they fire about one each. Two causes, and only one of
  them is the scale: a 1,000 m kill ring covers the whole cluster
  where an 85 m one covered only the mount being attacked, and pad
  egress - now wired, four ticks per airframe - means a tap is no
  longer one instant. 'Launched together' is a tap, not a wave.

  Against a tap the stack still does not pay. Four mounts read within
  a couple of points of one mount in every column, for 450 Materiel
  each, and the rounds column says why: the lead fires its whole belt
  either way and each supporting mount gets about one round, because
  it commits to a target that is already being engaged and the
  engagement is over before it can commit to another.

  Against a stream it does. Four mounts take six points off the
  attack at three drones and twelve at six, and every supporting
  mount fires four and a half rounds rather than one. That is the
  whole content of FINDINGS 13's structural argument and it is the
  first table in this project to show it, because it is the first one
  to offer a second engagement for a second mount to service.

  One row does not fit and is left standing rather than smoothed. In
  the stream arm TWO mounts are worse for the defence than one, in
  all four columns - 22 against 17, 48 against 37, 71 against 67, 90
  against 96 - while three and four are better than either. The cells
  share seeds, so the comparison is paired and the standard error is
  smaller than the five points 100 trials would suggest, but four
  cells one way is suggestive rather than settled. If it is real it is
  about engagement commitment - a supporting mount that kills the
  drone the lead had committed to costs the lead an acquisition - and
  it is worth a probe rather than a paragraph.
