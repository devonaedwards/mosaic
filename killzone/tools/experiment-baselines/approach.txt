  A forward pad is worth something, and it is worth much less than
  the map makes it look. Moving the pad from 5,400 m to 300 m - most
  of a map width - takes the mount from surviving one attack in five
  to surviving none, and nearly all of that is spent in the last
  900 m. Above the kill ring the column barely moves, because what
  the mount spends is not the crossing time - it has thirty real
  seconds of that at every row but the last - it is the seconds
  between latching a track and the drone arriving, and those are set
  by the ring and not by the pad.

  The two rows either side of the ring are the defence's best after
  the longest standoff, by about two standard errors at 200 trials.
  A pad at the rim gives the mount its engagement at maximum range
  and its full belt to spend on the approach; a pad inside the rim
  gives it neither. Worth a probe before it is worth a conclusion.

  The arrived column is flat across the whole sweep and that is the
  more useful half: a forward pad does not get more drones through, it
  gets the same drones through against a mount that has had less
  warning. The old pad set - chosen around the mount's long-since-
  corrected 550-compressed-metre reach - could show neither.
