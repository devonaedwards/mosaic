  The first four rows do not move across the offset columns at all,
  and that is the result rather than a broken table: against anything
  under about 50 m/s an 85 m/s interceptor closes by pursuit whatever
  it was told and from wherever it started, so where the pad sits is
  worth nothing. They do move between the arms - 53% with a mast and
  40% without, in every cell - and that 13 points is the cue
  multiplier and not the lead: the interceptor was going to arrive
  either way, and what the mast changes is the roll it makes when it
  gets there.

  The Multirole Quad is the exception and it is a signature and not a
  speed. Its radar cross-section is 26 against the Heavy Strike Drone's
  52, and the mast does not hold it at the merge at all, so its two
  arms are the same number. A radar mast is not air defence for small
  drones. It is air defence for big ones, which is the roster saying
  out loud what it has always implied.

  The jet row is the experiment. Read it two ways. Across, the offset
  columns fall away to nothing: a pad off the raid's track has to make
  the lateral distance up out of a closing budget it does not have, and
  at 3,000 m off it never arrives. Down, the two arms differ by about
  a third at every offset the interceptor can reach at all, and that
  gap is the mast: a radar track flies the whole computed lead and
  scores the merge at 1.00, an optical one flies 0.55 of it and scores
  at 0.60. Against a 140 m/s target the missing 45% of the lead is most
  of a kilometre of aimpoint.

  So the sentence the roster has always implied is now measured: the
  interceptor is a good buy against everything it can catch and a
  coin-toss against the one thing it was bought for, and the coin is
  weighted by whether a mast is up.
