  The first four rows do not move across the offset columns at all,
  and that is the result rather than a broken table: against anything
  under about 50 m/s an 85 m/s interceptor closes by pursuit whatever
  it was told and from wherever it started, so where the pad sits is
  worth nothing. They do move between the arms - 53% with a mast and
  40% without, in every cell - and that 13 points is the cue
  multiplier and not the lead: the interceptor was going to arrive
  either way, and what the mast changes is the roll it makes when it
  gets there.

  The Multirole Quad is the exception and it is a signature and not a
  speed. Its radar cross-section is 26 against the Heavy Strike Drone's
  52, and the mast does not hold it at the merge at all, so its two
  arms are the same number. A radar mast is not air defence for small
  drones. It is air defence for big ones, which is the roster saying
  out loud what it has always implied.

  Read the last three rows down the page and the cliff FINDINGS 41
  reported is a slope. With a mast: 53, 35, 25. Without one: 40, 18,
  16. The 90 m/s rung is new and it is the whole reason this table now
  says anything about *how fast* rather than only about fast or slow.

  And the mast is worth most in the middle, which nothing predicted.
  The gap between the arms is 13 points at 50 m/s and under, 17 at 90,
  and 9 at 140 - as a multiplier, 1.33, 1.94 and 1.56. Against a slow
  target a radar track is worth a better roll at a merge that was
  happening anyway. Against the jet it is worth a great deal per merge
  and there are fewer merges to have it at. At 90 m/s both apply: the
  interceptor still reaches, and the lead it flies decides the merge.
  A radar mast is not bought against the fastest thing in the sky. It
  is bought against the fastest thing an interceptor can still catch.

  The jet row is still the extreme, and the offsets are where it shows.
  Across, the offset columns fall away to nothing: a pad off the raid's
  track has to make the lateral distance up out of a closing budget it
  does not have. Where that happens is now a gradient too, and it is a
  gradient in two variables at once - at 50 m/s and under the pad may
  sit anywhere; at 90 it may sit anywhere with a mast up and no further
  than 1,800 m off without one; at 140 it is 1,800 m with a mast and
  900 m without. The mast does not only improve the shot. It widens the
  ground you are allowed to have put the pad on, and the faster the
  target the more of that width it is buying.

  Arm (c) is the same slope with the vectoring taken out of it: 100%,
  78%, 46% at 50, 90 and 140 m/s against a mount that simply shoots.
  Read with the arms above, that is the separation worth having - the
  gun degrades smoothly with speed and the interceptor does not, and
  the interceptor's cliff is about arriving rather than about hitting.

  Caveat, in the table's own terms: 60 trials carry about six points of
  standard error. The differences read above are 9 to 18 points and the
  ones deliberately not read - 96 against 98 in arm (c), 35 against 35
  across an offset row - are inside it.
